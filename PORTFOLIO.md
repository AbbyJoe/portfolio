## 2. What I do at Midddleman

Website: https://www.midddleman.com/

I'm a software engineer and technical leader at Midddleman, where I help lead engineering and build technology for cross-border trade between Africa and China.

The problem we're solving is simple to describe but complicated to execute.

A business wants to buy goods from China. Finding the right products is one thing. Paying for them is another. Then there's getting them shipped, navigating logistics, and making sure everything arrives as expected.

We're building technology to make that entire process feel a lot less complicated — helping African businesses source products, make payments, and manage the journey from supplier to destination.

I enjoy the challenge of taking a process with many moving parts and making it feel almost ordinary for the person using it.

### My role

I help lead the engineering team and contribute to technical direction, architecture, product development, and engineering delivery.

My work involves thinking about how different parts of the platform fit together, how services communicate, how integrations should work, and how to build systems that can grow with the business.

Midddleman has also been a place where I've had to think beyond writing code — about infrastructure, reliability, technical decisions, and how engineering supports the business.

### What we're building

Midddleman has evolved from an escrow-focused product into a broader procurement and trade platform.

The technology spans product discovery and sourcing, supplier interactions, payments, and logistics.

One of the interesting technical challenges has been integrating Chinese product sourcing into a platform that African businesses can actually use. This includes making product information more accessible and helping bridge the gaps between discovering a product, paying for it, and getting it delivered.

The bigger picture is helping African businesses participate more effectively in global trade, particularly the Africa–China trade corridor.

## 6. How I think about engineering

I believe good engineering is about understanding the problem, making thoughtful decisions, and building something that works reliably for the people who depend on it.

I'm also interested in how AI is changing software engineering — particularly how engineers can use AI effectively while continuing to understand the systems they're building.

I don't think engineering stops being important just because AI can generate code. Understanding what to build, why to build it, and whether it actually works still matters.
